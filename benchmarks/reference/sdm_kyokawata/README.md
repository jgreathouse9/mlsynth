# SDM — Kyo and Kawata (2026), sequential deviation minimization

A pre-build assessment spike for the SDM strategy
([doi:10.1080/02664763.2026.2717163](https://doi.org/10.1080/02664763.2026.2717163),
*Journal of Applied Statistics*), a proposed refinement of synthetic control
that builds donor weights by rescaling one donor at a time until the update
coefficients settle near one. No estimator has been added to `mlsynth`. The
verdict is pass, and this bundle is the evidence, kept so the assessment is not
repeated. The roadmap entry is item 22 of `agents/future_integrations.md`.

The short version: SDM's fixed point is MSCc — free intercept, non-negative
donor weights, no adding-up constraint — which `mlsynth.TSSC` already fits
exactly with a convex solve. SDM is a greedy approximation to that solve, so it
cannot beat it, and on the authors' own demo panel it does not. Separately, the
released script does not implement the article's Equation (14), and the gap
costs an order of magnitude of pre-period fit.

`run_simulation.py` needs nothing external: the Monte Carlo design is fully
specified in Section 4.1. `run_demo.py` needs `data_demo_F.txt` from the
authors' package, which is not committed here (see Provenance). The committed
`results.json` records the numbers from a run against it.

## The method, and what it reduces to

SDM centres each unit's pre-period outcome, keeps `w_j >= 0`, drops the
sum-to-one constraint, initialises from clamped univariate slopes, and then
rescales one donor at a time by the least-squares coefficient of the current
residual on that donor's contribution. The intercept is restored at the end as
`alpha = mean(y_1) - sum_j w_j mean(y_j)`.

Concentrating the intercept out of `min_{alpha, w >= 0} ||y_1 - alpha - Y_0 w||^2`
gives exactly the centred problem SDM solves, so the two are the same objective:
MSCc in the taxonomy of Li and Shankar (2023), and `TSSCConfig.method="MSCc"` in
this library (`mlsynth/utils/tssc_helpers/estimation.py`).

SDM approaches it by coordinate descent, with one deviation. The update is
multiplicative, `w_j <- a_j w_j`, so a weight driven to zero — or one whose
initial univariate slope was non-positive — can never come back. Ordinary
coordinate descent lifts a coordinate off the boundary when the gradient later
favours it; SDM cannot. The result is non-negative least squares on a support
chosen greedily and irreversibly, whose objective value is weakly worse than
MSCc's, with equality only when the greedy support happens to contain the MSCc
optimal support. Section 3.2.4 of the article concedes that no optimality
follows from the construction.

## The released script does not implement Equation (14)

The iteration converges to weights summing to 0.6307 on the demo panel. The
final block then does `w <- Sbet/sum(Sbet)`, renormalising to sum to one, and
builds the counterfactual from that. The article describes no such step, and
Section 3.2.1 states the design premise as allowing the sum of the weights to
deviate from unity. The renormalisation discards the relaxation the method is
about.

| weight vector | sum | pre-period MSPE | ATT |
|---|---|---|---|
| what the iteration optimises | 0.6307 | 4,632,462 | 51,078 |
| what the script ships | 1.0000 | 45,733,960 | 66,880 |

The script prints both — `Final MSPE` from the first, `RMSE (pre-treatment)`
from the second — and they disagree by 3.1x in RMSE terms. Section 5.2 of the
article reports the MSPE number (4,879,063 for its own application), which comes
from the pre-renormalisation object; Table 2's weights sum to 0.9938, consistent
with the post-renormalisation object. The reported fit statistic and the
reported weights appear to describe two different estimators, and the treatment
effect comes from the worse one.

A second inconsistency is benign. `X` is built with `bet/sum(bet)` while the
bookkeeping vector is initialised `bet/M`, so the two differ by a constant
factor of 4.3308 throughout; the final normalisation cancels it.

Figure 4(a) cannot discriminate between the two: the script forces the y-axis to
start at zero, so on a series of level 3e5 a 2,152 RMSE and a 6,763 RMSE look
alike.

## Demo panel, all arms exact

47 Japanese prefectures, 1999–2023, treated unit Fukui, intervention 2015,
`T0 = 16`, 46 donors. This is not the article's application (Iwate, 2011,
registered dogs), whose data the package does not include, so the article's own
Table 2 and Figure 4 cannot be checked directly.

| arm | sum w | pre-MSPE | ATT |
|---|---|---|---|
| `TSSC(method="MSCc")` via mlsynth | 0.6325 | 4,370,135 | 50,746 |
| MSCc exact (scipy) | 0.6326 | 4,370,135 | 50,746 |
| MSCb exact | 0.6384 | 4,468,136 | 52,162 |
| SDM objective | 0.6307 | 4,632,462 | 51,078 |
| MSCa exact | 1.0000 | 6,778,472 | 50,568 |
| SC exact | 1.0000 | 9,390,726 | 46,482 |
| SDM shipped | 1.0000 | 45,733,960 | 66,880 |

The library arm and the standalone solve agree to the digit, so the claim that
mlsynth already fits SDM's fixed point is checked against `mlsynth.TSSC` and not
against a stand-in.

Two readings. Every arm that fits well agrees on an ATT near 50.6k–51.1k, SDM's
own internal objective included, so the method lands essentially where MSCc
lands — which is what the convexity argument predicts, and MSCc still edges it
by 6 percent. And the shipped estimator is 6.7x worse than plain SC, the
baseline the article sets out to beat, with an ATT inflated by a third.

## The convergence index is not Equation (13)

`NO <- which(a > 0)` operates on `a`, a scalar holding the last accepted update
from the inner loop, not the vector over the substantial set `G`. So the
stopping statistic is `(a_last - 1)^2` for whichever donor was updated last.

On the demo panel the run stops at sweep 30 with the coded rho at 9.25e-05, just
under the 1e-4 threshold, while Equation (13) over the substantial set stands at
4.39e-03 — 47 times the threshold, with 8 donors still being actively updated.
The run terminates because one leftover scalar landed near one. Figure 3's
convergence index is that scalar.

`Reg1` also leaves `V` unassigned when a donor column has been zeroed, and R
resolves it lexically to the `V` left in the global environment by the previous
inner-loop step, so a run can consume a stale objective with no diagnostic. It
fires zero times on this panel, so it is a hazard here and not an active fault.
`sdm_port.RScope` emulates it and counts.

## Section 4's Monte Carlo, with a baseline

The design sets `y_jt = w_j F_t + e_jt` with the treated loading at 1 and the
donor loadings summing to one, which Section 4.1 reads as a courtesy to
conventional SC. A convex combination of donors reaches loading
`sum_j v_j w_j <= max_j w_j`, of order `1.5/J`, against a treated loading of 1.
The simplex arm cannot track the factor, and its deficit grows with `J` because
the donor loadings shrink.

300 replications, `T0 = 20`, seed 0:

| J | SC | MSCb | MSCc | reachable loading | needed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 4.2414 | 1.9439 | 1.6431 | 0.3070 | 1.0 |
| 10 | 5.0289 | 2.2205 | 1.6420 | 0.1330 | 1.0 |
| 20 | 5.2520 | 1.9069 | 1.3477 | 0.0707 | 1.0 |
| 50 | 5.3671 | 0.2444 | 0.1357 | 0.0294 | 1.0 |

The article reports SC at 4.4540 for `J = 5` rising to 5.4300 for `J = 50`, and
its own method flat at 1.7000–1.9100. The SC arm reproduces. Plain non-negative
least squares — no sequential updating, no convergence index — lands in the band
the article claims for its method, and MSCc beats it. Figure 1(b) separates
constrained from unconstrained, not sequential from joint.

Two further properties of the design. Section 4.1 measures RMSPE on the
pre-period only, so it scores in-sample fit with nothing held out. And at
`J = 50` with `T0 = 20` the relaxed arms carry more free parameters than
observations and drive that error toward zero, which makes the metric degenerate
at the largest `J` reported.

## What the package does not contain

Of the article's three contributions, the package implements one. There is no
scale-adjustment parameter `theta` anywhere: `code_demo_F_co.txt` stacks raw
centred covariates under the outcome, which is Section 3.3 with `theta` fixed at
1, and the estimate `theta = argmin MSPE(theta)` is never formed. There is no
cross-validation, so the standardised diagnostic statistics of Section 3.4 and
Figure 5 are not reproducible from it. There is no simulation code.

## If this is revisited

The thing that would change the verdict is a performance claim, not an accuracy
one. Fix the final block and SDM is a reasonable approximate MSCc solver that
agrees with the exact solve on the ATT, built from inner products with no convex
solver in the loop. If a large-`J` panel ever makes the cvxpy path in `TSSC` a
bottleneck, this is the shape of the alternative — a `TSSCConfig.solver="sdm"`
option, not a new estimator. Nothing in the current benchmark suite is
solver-bound, so that case has not arisen.

The idea the article gets right and does not resolve is in Section 3.1.2, insight
4: placebo inference assumes the construction procedure transfers across units,
and scale mismatch breaks that assumption. The answer offered is a statistic
whose denominator is the number of resamples, so it grows with computational
effort. If someone calibrates that idea, review the result.

The replication package was supplied by the authors; the article states only
that code is available on request, and the package is not published anywhere.
